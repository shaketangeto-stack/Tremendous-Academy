Introduction

Email is an important form of written communication used in education, workplaces and everyday communication. In this lesson, learners explore the structure and purpose of emails and learn how to select appropriate language and register for different communication situations.

Learners will focus particularly on writing a clear, appropriately formatted formal email and on editing their writing for grammar, spelling and punctuation.

Prior Knowledge

Learners are expected to know:

  • Basic computer and/or mobile communication.
  • The parts of a letter.
  • Formal and informal language.

General Objective

Learners will write a variety of texts using effective word choice, sentence structure and forms.

Specific Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

1. Define an email and explain its purpose.
2. Identify the main parts of an email.
3. Distinguish between formal and informal emails.
4. Use appropriate formal language and register.
5. Organise ideas logically in an email.
6. Write a clear and appropriately formatted formal email.
7. Edit and proofread an email for grammar, spelling and punctuation errors.

2. Learning Content

What Is an Email?

An email is an electronic message sent from one person or organisation to another using an electronic communication system. Emails can be used for personal, educational or professional communication.

Purpose of an Email

The purpose of an email depends on the situation and the relationship between the sender and recipient. An email may be used to communicate information, make a request, ask a question, provide an explanation, or maintain contact.

Main Parts of an Email

  1. Recipient: The person or organisation receiving the email.
  2. Subject: A short statement showing what the email is about.
  3. Greeting: The opening expression used to address the recipient.
  4. Body: The main message containing the information, request or explanation.
  5. Closing: The expression used to end the message before the sender's name.

Formal and Informal Emails

Formal Email Informal Email
Used when communicating in a formal, educational or professional situation. Usually used when communicating with people with whom the writer has a familiar relationship.
Uses polite and appropriate formal language. May use more relaxed and conversational language.
Avoids unnecessary slang and overly casual expressions. May contain more familiar expressions.
Ideas should be clearly organised and professionally presented. The style may be more personal and conversational.

Formal Language and Register

Register refers to the style of language appropriate for a particular situation and audience. When writing a formal email, learners should consider who will receive the message and select respectful and appropriate language.

Remember: Before sending an email, check the recipient, subject, greeting, body, closing, spelling, grammar and punctuation.

3. ICT Teaching Aids

The lesson plan identifies a laptop with a drafted email and a projector for displaying the email sample as learning and teaching media.

💻

Teaching Aid 1: Drafted Email on a Laptop

The teacher can prepare a model email on a laptop and use it to demonstrate the structure of an email and the appropriate use of formal language.

Learning purpose: Learners can examine the different parts of the email and observe how information is organised.

📽️

Teaching Aid 2: Projector

The teacher can display the model email on a projector so that the class can view and discuss its structure and language together.

Learning purpose: The projected example allows the teacher to highlight the recipient, subject, greeting, body and closing while learners follow the explanation.

Model Formal Email

To: principal@school.example
Subject: Request for Permission to Hold a School Event

Dear Principal,

I am writing to respectfully request permission to hold a school event for learners at our school. The event would provide learners with an opportunity to participate in an educational and social activity.

I would appreciate your consideration of this request. I am available to provide further information about the proposed event if required.

Thank you for considering my request.

Yours faithfully,
Student Name

Note: The email above is a model for demonstrating structure, organisation and formal register. Learners should produce their own wording in the assessment task.

5. Interactive Learning Activity

Write a Formal Email to the Principal

Imagine that you want to organise a school event. You need permission from the principal before the event can take place.

Instructions

  1. Write an appropriate subject line.
  2. Use a suitable formal greeting.
  3. Clearly explain why you are writing.
  4. Give relevant information about the proposed event.
  5. Make your request politely.
  6. Use an appropriate formal closing.
  7. Proofread your email for grammar, spelling and punctuation.

6. Digital Assessment

Complete Questions 1–5 (one mark each) and Question 6, the written email (20 marks). Questions 1–5 are marked automatically; the teacher marks Question 6 using the memorandum below. Total: 25 marks.

Question 1 — 1 mark

Which part of an email tells the recipient what the message is about?

Question 2 — 1 mark

Which language style is most appropriate when writing a formal email to a principal?

Question 3 — 1 mark

Which of the following is normally part of the body of an email?

Question 4 — 1 mark

Why should a learner proofread a formal email before sending it?

Question 5 — 1 mark

Which closing is most appropriate for a formal email when the recipient’s name is known?

Question 6 — 20 marks

Write a formal email to the principal requesting permission for a school event.

Your email should include an appropriate subject, greeting, clearly organised body and closing. Use appropriate formal language and proofread your work.

7. Memorandum and Marking Rubric

Multiple-Choice Answer Key (5 marks)

  1. Question 1: B — Subject. The subject tells the recipient what the email is about.
  2. Question 2: A — Respectful and formal language. A formal email to a principal should use polite, appropriate language.
  3. Question 3: A — The main message or information. The body contains the main content of the email.
  4. Question 4: B — To check grammar, spelling and punctuation. Proofreading helps improve accuracy and clarity.
  5. Question 5: C — Yours sincerely. This is an appropriate formal closing when the recipient is addressed by name.

Question 6: Written Formal Email (20 marks)

Expected response: a clear formal email to the principal requesting permission to hold a school event. Accept any relevant, coherent response that fulfils the task and uses an appropriate email format.

9. Learning Support

Support for learners who need guidance

  • Use the model formal email to identify the subject, greeting, body and closing.
  • Plan the main ideas in the interactive activity before writing the complete email.
  • Use the checklist to confirm that the request is polite and the ideas are organised.
  • Proofread the final email for grammar, spelling and punctuation.

Extension for confident writers

Add a concise explanation of the event’s educational value, include relevant practical details, and revise the email to make it clear, concise and professional.
